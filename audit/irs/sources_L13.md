## [BA08] S. Baluja, R. Seth, D. Sivakumar, Y. Jing, J. Yagnik, S. Kumar, D. Ravichandran, M. Aly — Video Suggestion and Discovery for YouTube: Taking Random Walks Through the View Graph. WWW 2008, Beijing.
URL: https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/34407.pdf (bản của Google Research)
- "It is estimated that there are over 45,000,000 videos in the repository [14], and that the collection is growing at an astounding rate of seven hours of video being uploaded every minute [6]."
- "The Netflix domain has under 100,000 DVDs, and Netflix users provide a large number of explicitly given ratings on the videos. In contrast, for our task, the number of videos is significantly larger, and the ratings are sparse."
- "In the simplest form, the co-view data gives, for any pair of videos, the number of people who viewed both videos."
- "Imagine that User U watches two videos, J and K. From our co-view statistics, we know that many other users who saw video J also saw videos L, M, N. Similarly, for video K, we know that many other users saw videos N, O, P, Q. Therefore, the videos we may recommend to U , based on his watches of J and K can simply be the union of the two co-view sets: L, M, N, O, P, Q. ... (notice that Video N was recommended twice)"
- "The raw data for our experiments was collected from live user views of videos from youtube.com over 92-day period from July 1, 2007, through September 30, 2007. We picked a sample of approximately 5.4 million users from a specific geographic area ... For each of the chosen users, we collected the list of all videos they watched beyond the 33% mark ... This resulted in nearly 29 million total views of a set of approximately 4.2 million videos"
- "a training set consisting of all watches from the first 46 days of the evaluation period, and a test set consisting of all watches from the remaining days"
- "After these operations, we kept approximately 1.1 million users and 1.3 million videos, with approximately 12.5 million watches."
- "(1) Hindsight is not 20/20: If a suggestion is made to a user that the user did not view in the evaluation period, marking it incorrect is not necessarily appropriate. If the recommendation had been shown, the user might have watched it."
- "testing all of them in live service would have been impractical. The need for running each variation with enough users for a long enough time to obtain statistically significant differences conflicts with the need to ensure that all of our users get a consistent, good, experience on YouTube."
- "The first, most basic, reference algorithm is called GP, for global popularity."
- "With its top one recommendation, Algorithm GP achieves roughly 8.4% precision at 3.2% recall, and with its top 100 recommendations, a recall of 10.6% at a precision of 0.6%."
- "For most values of precision, we observe a gain of 17% in recall over LP, and for most values of recall, we observe 21% improvement in precision over LP."
- (Hình 5) "This suggests that for very casual users (who visit a system infrequently), recommending the most popular videos is a useful strategy." ; "LP is quite poor for users with a very small number of views (nearly 20–25% worse than GP)."

## [CO16] P. Covington, J. Adams, E. Sargin — Deep Neural Networks for YouTube Recommendations. RecSys 2016, Boston.
URL: https://static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/45530.pdf
- "YouTube recommendations are responsible for helping more than a billion users discover personalized content from an ever-growing corpus of videos."
- "Our models learn approximately one billion parameters and are trained on hundreds of billions of examples."
- Hình 2: "video corpus — millions — candidate generation — hundreds — ranking — dozens".
- "The candidate generation network ... retrieves a small subset (hundreds) of videos from a large corpus."
- "Furthermore, this design enables blending candidates generated by other sources, such as those described in an earlier work [3]."
- "During development, we make extensive use of offline metrics (precision, recall, ranking loss, etc.) ... However for the final determination of the effectiveness of an algorithm or model, we rely on A/B testing via live experiments. ... This is important because live A/B results are not always correlated with offline experiments."
- "The predecessor to the recommender described here was a matrix factorization approach trained under rank loss [23]."
- "we use the implicit feedback [16] of watches to train the model, where a user completing a video is a positive example."
- "In practice several thousand negatives are sampled, corresponding to more than 100 times speedup over traditional softmax."
- "Scoring millions of items under a strict serving latency of tens of milliseconds requires an approximate scoring scheme sublinear in the number of classes."
- "In these experiments, a vocabulary of 1M videos and 1M search tokens were embedded with 256 floats each in a maximum bag size of 50 recent watches and 50 recent searches."
- "Training examples are generated from all YouTube watches (even those embedded on other sites) rather than just watches on the recommendations we produce. Otherwise, it would be very difficult for new content to surface and the recommender would be overly biased towards exploitation."
- "generate a fixed number of training examples per user, effectively weighting our users equally in the loss function. This prevented a small cohort of highly active users from dominating the loss."
- "Ranking by click-through rate often promotes deceptive videos that the user does not complete ("clickbait") whereas watch time better captures engagement"
- "the positive (clicked) impressions are weighted by the observed watch time on the video. Negative (unclicked) impressions all receive unit weight."
- Bảng 1: "1024 ReLU → 512 ReLU → 256 ReLU — 34.6%" ; "we also trained a model where positive and negative examples are weighted equally. Unsurprisingly, this increased the watch time-weighted loss by a dramatic 4.1%."
- "The configuration of a 1024-wide ReLU followed by a 512-wide ReLU followed by a 256-wide ReLU gave us the best results while enabling us to stay within our serving CPU budget."

## [ZH19] Z. Zhao, L. Hong, L. Wei, J. Chen, A. Nath, S. Andrews, A. Kumthekar, M. Sathiamoorthy, X. Yi, E. Chi — Recommending What Video to Watch Next: A Multitask Ranking System. RecSys 2019, Copenhagen.
URL: https://daiwk.github.io/assets/youtube-multitask.pdf (bản chép cá nhân; dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3298689.3346997 trả trang chặn Cloudflare; đã đối chiếu tiêu đề, tác giả, DOI, số trang 43–51 in trong bản chép).
- "a user might have clicked and watched a video simply because it was being ranked high, not because it was the one that the user liked the most. Therefore, models trained using data generated from the current system will be biased, causing a feedback loop effect [33]."

- "YouTube is the largest video sharing platform, with 1.9 billion monthly active users. The website creates hundreds of billions of user logs everyday"
- "we propose to add a shallow tower to the main model ... The shallow tower takes input related to the selection bias, e.g., ranking order decided by the current system, and outputs a scalar serving as a bias term to the final prediction"
- "In training, the positions of all impressions are used, with a 10% feature drop-out rate to prevent our model from over-relying on the position feature. At serving time, position feature is treated as missing."
- Bảng 2 (thử trực tuyến, thiên lệch vị trí): "Input Feature −0.07% ; Adversarial Loss +0.01% ; Shallow Tower +0.24%".
- Bảng 1 (MMoE): "Shared-Bottom 6.1M: +0.1% / +1.89% ; MMoE (4 experts): +0.20% / +1.22% ; MMoE (8 Experts): +0.45% / +3.07%" (cột tương tác / hài lòng, so với gốc 3.7M phép nhân).
- "often times we observe misalignment between offline and online metrics. Therefore, it is preferable to choose an overall simpler model"

## [CH19] M. Chen, A. Beutel, P. Covington, S. Jain, F. Belletti, E. Chi — Top-K Off-Policy Correction for a REINFORCE Recommender System. WSDM 2019, Melbourne.
URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.02353 (bản v3)
- "Industrial recommender systems deal with extremely large action spaces – many millions of items to recommend. Moreover, they need to serve billions of users"
- "Learning from the logged feedback is however subject to biases caused by only observing feedback on recommendations selected by the previous versions of the recommender."
- "the Netflix Prize dataset was only 0.1% dense [5]"
- "brute-force exploration, such as ε-greedy, is not viable in a production system like YouTube where this could, and mostly likely would, result in inappropriate recommendations and a bad user experience."
- "we split users on the platform into three buckets: 90%, 5%, 5%. ... the last bucket of users is served with a stochastic policy based on a model trained with exploratory data."
- "we observe a statistically significant increase in ViewTime by 0.07% in the test population. While the improvement is not large, it comes from a relatively small amount of exploration data (only 5% of users experience the stochastic policy)."
- "Smaller value of c reduces variance in the gradient estimate, but introduces larger bias." (công thức (9): trọng số = min(π/β, c))
- "We compare a model trained using cap c = 3 (as in production model) ... with one trained using c = 5. ... we observe a significant drop of 0.52% in ViewTime when the cap on importance weight was lifted."
- "In this experiment, we use K = 16 and capping c = 3 ... we find that the amount of ViewTime increased by 0.85% in the test traffic, with the number of videos viewed slightly decreasing by 0.16%."

## [YI19] X. Yi, J. Yang, L. Hong, D. Cheng, L. Heldt, A. Kumthekar, Z. Zhao, L. Wei, E. Chi — Sampling-Bias-Corrected Neural Modeling for Large Corpus Item Recommendations. RecSys 2019, Copenhagen.
URL: https://storage.googleapis.com/gweb-research2023-media/pubtools/5716.pdf (bản của Google Research)
- "The system is deployed to retrieve personalized suggestions from a corpus with tens of millions of videos."
- "in-batch loss is subject to sampling biases ... popular items are overly penalized as negatives due to the high probability of being included in a batch."
- "we correct each logit s(x_i, y_j) by the following equation s^c(x_i, y_j) = s(x_i, y_j) − log(p_j). Here p_j denotes the sampling probability of item j in a random batch."
- "For example, if one item gets sampled every 50 steps, then we have p = 0.02."
- "At nomination stage, we have multiple nominators that each generates hundreds of video recommendations ... In this section, we focus on building an additional nominator in the retrieval stage"
- "In YouTube, new training data is generated every day, and training datasets are accordingly organized by days. ... Once the trainer has caught up to the latest day of training data, it waits for the next day's training data to arrive."
- "index of approximately 10M videos chosen from the YouTube corpus is built periodically every few hours. The index corpus is subject to change over time due to, for example, uploads of fresh videos. But it typically covers more than 90% of training examples."
- "The YouTube training data we use includes billions of clicked videos on a daily basis, across many days."
- "As recommending videos which users like to click is not ideal, for live experiments we train the model with the reward in a way to reflect the users' real engagement with clicked videos."
- Bảng 3: "plain-sfx τ = 0.05: +0.20% ; correct-sfx τ = 0.05: +0.37%".
