## [BT06] F. Chang, J. Dean, S. Ghemawat, W. C. Hsieh, D. A. Wallach, M. Burrows, T. Chandra, A. Fikes, R. E. Gruber. *Bigtable: A Distributed Storage System for Structured Data.* OSDI 2006
Tải: static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//archive/bigtable-osdi06.pdf (14 trang; sha256 9126cf3b930fd7be…).
- Tóm tắt: “designed to scale to a very large size: petabytes of data across thousands of commodity servers.”
- §1: “Over the last two and a half years we have designed, implemented, and deployed a distributed storage system for managing structured data at Google called Bigtable.” “used by more than sixty Google products and projects”; clusters “from a handful to thousands of servers, and store up to several hundred terabytes of data.”
- §2: “A Bigtable is a sparse, distributed, persistent multidimensional sorted map. The map is indexed by a row key, column key, and a timestamp”.
- §2 Rows: “Every read or write of data under a single row key is atomic”; “Each row range is called a tablet, which is the unit of distribution and load balancing.” Webtable: “store data for maps.google.com/index.html under the key com.google.maps/index.html”.
- §2 (API): “Bigtable supports single-row transactions … Bigtable does not currently support general transactions across row keys”.
- §5: “each tablet server manages … between ten to a thousand tablets per tablet server”; “each approximately 100-200 MB in size by default.”
- §5.1: “a three-level hierarchy analogous to that of a B+-tree”; “Each METADATA row stores approximately 1KB of data in memory. With a modest limit of 128 MB METADATA tablets, our three-level location scheme is sufficient to address 2^34 tablets (or 2^61 bytes in 128 MB tablets).” “If the client’s cache is empty, the location algorithm requires three network round-trips, including one read from Chubby.”
- §4: Chubby “consists of five active replicas … live when a majority of the replicas are running”; “The average percentage of Bigtable server hours during which some data stored in Bigtable was not available due to Chubby unavailability … was 0.0047%. The percentage for the single cluster that was most affected by Chubby unavailability was 0.0326%.” (đo trên “14 Bigtable clusters spanning 11 Chubby instances”).
- §8: “As of August 2006, there are 388 non-test Bigtable clusters … with a combined total of about 24,500 tablet servers.” “One group of 14 busy clusters with 8069 total tablet servers saw an aggregate volume of more than 1.2 million requests per second”.
- §8.3: Personalized Search “originally built a client-side replication mechanism on top of Bigtable that ensured eventual consistency of all replicas.”
- §9: “large distributed systems are vulnerable to many types of failures, not just the standard network partitions and fail-stop failures” (memory and network corruption, large clock skew, hung machines …).
- §9: “it is important to delay adding new features until it is clear how the new features will be used. For example, we initially planned to support general-purpose transactions in our API. Because we did not have an immediate use for them, however, we did not implement them. … most applications require only single-row transactions. Where people have requested distributed transactions, the most important use is for maintaining secondary indices”.
- §9: “The most important lesson we learned is the value of simple designs.” (“about 100,000 lines of non-test code”).
- §11: “Bigtable clusters have been in production use since April 2005, and we spent roughly seven person-years on design and implementation before that date.”

## [SP12] J. C. Corbett, J. Dean, M. Epstein, A. Fikes, C. Frost, J. J. Furman, S. Ghemawat và cộng sự. *Spanner: Google’s Globally-Distributed Database.* OSDI 2012
Tải: static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//archive/spanner-osdi2012.pdf (14 trang; sha256 8cf30f4b379da587…).
(Chữ ε, ε̄ trong các câu dưới bị mất khi trích bằng pdftotext — điền lại theo định nghĩa ở §3: ε là nửa bề rộng khoảng, ε̄ là trung bình.)
- §1: “Even though many projects happily use Bigtable, we have also consistently received complaints from users that Bigtable can be difficult to use for some kinds of applications: those that have complex, evolving schemas, or those that want strong consistency in the presence of wide-area replication.” “Spanner has evolved from a Bigtable-like versioned key-value store into a temporal multi-version database.” “Spanner supports general-purpose transactions, and provides a SQL-based query language.”
- §1: “designed to scale up to millions of machines across hundreds of datacenters and trillions of database rows.” “Our initial customer was F1, a rewrite of Google’s advertising backend. F1 uses five replicas spread across the United States.”
- §1: external consistency: “if a transaction T1 commits before another transaction T2 starts, then T1’s commit timestamp is smaller than T2’s.” TrueTime “keeps uncertainty small (generally less than 10ms) by using multiple modern clock references (GPS and atomic clocks).” “If the uncertainty is large, Spanner slows down to wait out that uncertainty.”
- §2.1: “If a transaction involves more than one Paxos group, those groups’ leaders coordinate to perform two-phase commit.”
- §2.3: “At least 300 applications within Google use Megastore (despite its relatively low performance)”; “(Bigtable only supports eventually-consistent replication across datacenters.)”; “the lack of cross-row transactions in Bigtable led to frequent complaints”; “We believe it is better to have application programmers deal with performance problems due to overuse of transactions as bottlenecks arise, rather than always coding around the lack of transactions. Running two-phase commit over Paxos mitigates the availability problems.”
- §3: “TT.now() … TTinterval: [earliest, latest]”; “TT.after(t) true if t has definitely passed”; ε là nửa bề rộng khoảng. “ε is typically a sawtooth function of time, varying from about 1 to 7 ms over each poll interval. ε̄ is therefore 4 ms most of the time. The daemon’s poll interval is currently 30 seconds, and the current applied drift rate is set at 200 microseconds/second, which together account for the sawtooth bounds from 0 to 6 ms. The remaining 1 ms comes from the communication delay to the time masters.”
- §4.1.2 / §4.2.1: “The coordinator leader for a write Ti assigns a commit timestamp si no less than the value of TT.now().latest”; “Commit Wait: The coordinator leader ensures that clients cannot see any data committed by Ti until TT.after(si) is true.” “the expected wait is at least 2 ∗ ε̄. This wait is typically overlapped with Paxos communication.”
- §5.4: “Spanner started being experimentally evaluated under production workloads in early 2011, as part of a rewrite of Google’s advertising backend called F1. This backend was originally based on a MySQL database that was manually sharded many ways. The uncompressed dataset is tens of terabytes”; “The MySQL sharding scheme assigned each customer and all related data to a fixed shard.” “The last resharding took over two years of intense effort, and involved coordination and testing across dozens of teams to minimize risk. This operation was too complex to do regularly: as a result, the team had to limit growth on the MySQL database by storing some data in external Bigtables, which compromised transactional behavior and the ability to query across all data.”
- §5.4: “First, Spanner removes the need to manually reshard. Second, Spanner provides synchronous replication and automatic failover. With MySQL master-slave replication, failover was difficult, and risked data loss and downtime. Third, F1 requires strong transactional semantics”. “F1 has 2 replicas on the west coast of the US, and 3 on the east coast.” “Anecdotally, Spanner’s automatic failover has been nearly invisible to them.” “All application writes are now by default sent through F1 to Spanner”.
- §7: “We are currently implementing the Spanner schema language, automatic maintenance of secondary indices, and automatic load-based resharding.” “We see no insurmountable obstacle to reducing ε below 1ms.”
- §8: “Since Spanner’s inception, we have taken more than 5 years to iterate to the current design and implementation.” “the linchpin of Spanner’s feature set is TrueTime.”

## [SQ17] D. F. Bacon, N. Bales, N. Bruno, B. F. Cooper và cộng sự. *Spanner: Becoming a SQL System.* SIGMOD 2017, Chicago (doi 10.1145/3035918.3056103)
Tải: static.googleusercontent.com/media/research.google.com/en//pubs/archive/46103.pdf (sha256 d90704e4991786d9…).
- §1: “Google’s Spanner started out as a key-value store offering multirow transactions, external consistency, and transparent failover across datacenters. Over the past 7 years it has evolved into a relational database system.”
- §1: “Currently, over 5,000 databases run in our production instances”; “Spanner serves tens of millions of QPS across all of its databases, managing hundreds of petabytes of data.” “publicly available in beta as Cloud Spanner”.
- §8: “The original API of Spanner provided NoSQL methods for point lookups and range scans … While NoSQL methods provided a simple path to launching Spanner … SQL has provided significant additional value”. “In the year prior to releasing the common dialect, the SQL query volume increased by a factor of two. In the year after, the SQL query volume increased by a factor of five.”
- §8: “Internally, Spanner engineers spend a fraction of their time reviewing the schema designs for new internal applications. In many cases, schema reviews helped avoid production challenges later on.”
- §8: “Spanner has a very short development cycle, with new versions coming online on a roughly bi-weekly schedule. Given that Spanner backs mission-critical systems, performance regressions of any kind are extremely undesirable.”
- §8: “Since the OSDI’12 paper, Spanner continued improving its TrueTime epsilon”.
- §6 (kiểm thử): “The random query generation tool and the reference implementation are used together in unit tests and in system integration tests that run continuously generating millions of random queries per day.”
- §8: “Stabilizing the transactional NoSQL core and building a mature control plane took years of development effort, and involved addressing a myriad of extremely unlikely failure conditions that do eventually happen at scale.”

- [SP12] §1 (đối chiếu trang 1 bản PDF ngày 2026-11-08): “Most other applications will probably replicate their data across 3 to 5 datacenters in one geographic region, but with relatively independent failure modes.” → đây là DỰ ĐOÁN của nhóm tác giả, không phải số đo; bài nói “bài báo dự đoán”.

## Điều KHÔNG dạy (nguồn không ghi)
- Năm bắt đầu thiết kế Bigtable chính xác: [BT06] chỉ ghi “hai năm rưỡi” trước bài báo và “production since April 2005” → bài ghi mốc 2005 (chạy thật), không ghi năm bắt đầu.
- Số máy thật của F1 / số mảnh MySQL: [SP12] chỉ ghi “manually sharded many ways”, “tens of terabytes” → ví dụ 4 → 5 mảnh chia theo số dư là SỐ MINH HOẠ (dùng lại con số 80% của bài 6).
- Bigtable “không có giao dịch nên hỏng dữ liệu”: không nguồn nào ghi → không dạy; nguồn chỉ ghi khiếu nại của người dùng và việc phải “coding around the lack of transactions”.
- Commit wait đo được “about 5ms” ở bảng 3 [SP12] §5.1 (một bản sao, thí nghiệm vi mô) khác với chặn dưới lý thuyết 2ε̄ = 8 ms; bài chỉ dạy phép tính 2ε̄ từ cơ chế, không nêu 5 ms để khỏi lẫn hai đại lượng.
- Giá hay độ chính xác của đồng hồ nguyên tử, cách GPS hoạt động: ngoài phạm vi, không dạy.
- Cloud Spanner ra mắt chính thức (GA), con số sau 2017: chưa đọc nguồn chính thức → không dạy; chỉ nói “bản thử nghiệm công khai” theo [SQ17].
